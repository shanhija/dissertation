# Dissertation

The brief is [`design.md`](../design.md), the author's words: a party brawl in the spirit of A Gentlemen's Dispute, played by mad doctors who defend their theses literally, in first person 3D, for adults. Credibility is the health bar; an AI builds every arena; nothing is picked from a weapon stand, a player writes what they want and Opus makes it. Added in chat (2026-11-09): the character editor matters from the start, players dress while Opus builds each round's stage, and they can type how they want to look; a taunt button with silly, never-repeated lines spoken aloud; and things from crates can be disguises too (a box to hide in). How to run and play it is in [`README.md`](../README.md).

## Decisions

- **Friends at a LAN party; the host's computer is the server.** The game (sim, bots, made code) runs in the dev server; browsers send input and draw snapshots. Only local addresses may connect (loopback, 10/8, 172.16/12, 192.168/16, link-local, fc00::/7). `npm run dev` serves plain http on 3003: no certificate covers a LAN address.
- **Made code runs on the server, behind the guard** (`src/sim/guard.ts`). The guard allows only the code's own names, its parameters and plain JavaScript, refuses constructor and prototype keys, and puts a loop and time budget in every loop and function; each design gets a `node:vm` context of its own. The guard is the wall; the vm only keeps designs apart. Friends' words reach Opus, never the code.
- **Up to 8 players; bots only when asked for** (the chair's setting). Bots never ask Opus: they grab crates, get a crate filled from the archive every 25 to 45 s, and wear random parts from the wardrobe.
- **First to 5 rounds takes the match; no perks.**
- **Harm, enforced in `sim.ts`:** a hit takes at most 10; a thing takes at most its budget from a player in any 20 s, 10 for most and 20 for one hard to hit or risky ("most take just 10, but hard to hit (sniper) can have two rounds or risky (melee) can do 20"; at first the budget was for all its uses, and weapons felt like they had "1-2 charges", so on 2026-11-09 it was made to refill), and the thing prompt says things can be used again and again unless running out is what was asked for; one attacker takes at most 20 a second from another (the arena counts as one); with three or more others in, a thing never reaches all of them within a second. Bare hands shove: a push and 3. A thrown thing hits for 6 (against its budget). A fall out of the arena costs 10 and puts the doctor back at a spawn point. A hard landing costs 2.5 for each m/s over 12 (about 3.5 m of drop is free, 5 m costs about 4, from about 6.4 m up the 10 of one hit), put down to whoever hurt the doctor in the last 3 s (2026-11-10: "fall damage, yes"); a made thing that makes its holder jump higher raises the free speed by as much, so its own jumps cost nothing.
- **Holds** (with the weapons below): an effect may stop a doctor where they stand (speed 0, jump 0) or bind their hands (nothing used, picked up or thrown). Held for 5 s in a row, they shake free: the holds end and no new one takes for 2 s.
- **Asking:** one crate in the air per player and 15 s between requests; a typed request is always designed afresh. A crate opens within 30 s: a design not ready by then leaves the crate to a random thing from the archive, and is kept when it comes.
- **Models:** things and looks go to Opus while it keeps up; with 2 or more designs in flight the next goes to Sonnet, with 4 or more to Haiku, and a model that took over 22 s or failed is passed over for 2 minutes. Arenas always go to Opus (up to three tries with the checks' problems); taunts and titles to Haiku. All through the CLI driver (`src/server/claude.ts`), `--effort low`.
- **The voice (2026-11-10):** Kokoro-82M (Apache-2.0, ONNX fp32 through onnxruntime-node, phonemes by espeak-ng) because it is small enough for the host's CPU, needs no Python, has 28 English voices to mix and runs from `npm install` alone; more expressive models (Chatterbox, 0.5B, an exaggeration knob but one voice without reference recordings; NeuTTS-2E) need a GPU or Python to be quick. Measured on the development machine (i7-8750H, no CUDA libraries): a 6 s line takes 1.9 s on 4 threads (fp16 and q8 no faster, more threads no faster), so each player's next taunt is said ahead of V. onnxruntime holds its thread for the whole line (a 5 ms timer did not fire once in 1.6 s), so it runs in a worker thread. Kokoro's style is 128 numbers of timbre and 128 of prosody: Bella's prosody on George's timbre spoke at Bella's pitch (192 Hz against George's 142), Nicole's made speech slower and breathier, Santa's swung wider (5.6 semitones of spread against 3.8). Pushing a voice's prosody past itself (away from the mean) only slowed it, so tones pull toward other voices instead. espeak-ng's `en-gb` does not switch back from `en-us` within one process; `en` does. The men, said with English phonemes: Lewis 81 Hz, Onyx 88, Echo 109, Puck 113, Michael 114, Fable 114, Daniel 122, Liam 126, Fenrir 137, George 142, Santa 150, Eric 160 (Adam is graded F and left out). The women: Alloy 143, Nicole 151 (a whisper), Kore 155, Nova 164, Sky 166, River 178, Emma 180 (the flattest, 2.3 semitones of spread), Aoede 180, Isabella 197, Bella 198, Sarah 198, Heart 205, Lily 205, Jessica 209, Alice 211. Women's prosody on a man's timbre lifts him toward a woman's pitch (Emma's by 35 Hz), so each tone pulls toward voices of the doctor's own sex, but hushed, which takes Nicole's slow breathy delivery for both (about 10 Hz up for a man). Kokoro's own style can widen the melody (George with Onyx pushed from Daniel toward Santa: 3.5 to 6.6 semitones of spread) but lifts the whole voice with it (123 to 150 Hz), so the melody is widened afterwards by TD-PSOLA instead (`widen` in `voice-worker.ts`, about 0.1 s a line): at a swing of 1.8 George with Onyx went from 93..148 Hz (10th to 90th percentile) to 80..171 and Isabella with Kore from 157..242 to 135..299, the middle note kept, the periodicity a little lower (0.88 to 0.85). Without a limit a gloating man's highs reached 330 Hz, so notes stay within 9 semitones up and 8 down of the middle; then the men's highs top out about 220 Hz and the women's about 310.
- **Arenas are real places** (2026-11-10: "the arena should have structure, and not just a square box filled with obstacles", naming A Gentlemen's Dispute's places): the prompt asks for a real place as it truly is, its own shape, size and structure, the ways people move through it and what is always found there, and the fight coming from the place's own levels, corners, heights, edges and machinery, 40 to 90 m along its longest side. The examples given in chat stay out of the prompt (the rule for prompts). Academic places (learning, scholarship, science) by default; the chair can switch to anything at all. Every arena is checked before it is played: it builds, every spawn point has a floor, nobody standing at a spawn point is hurt within 2 s or falls out, every doctor can walk away from their spawn point, and a step takes under 6 ms. Doctors start facing the middle of the spawn points (Opus guessed the yaw convention wrong once, so it is not asked for). A kept arena is checked again before it is reused.
- **Movers carry and sweep:** a doctor standing on a mover goes with it, turning included (the mover's velocity at the doctor's own point), and a mover that moves into a doctor pushes them out along its motion with a knock as hard as it was going.
- **Arena pieces break** (2026-11-10: "can the environment be made to destruct when hit hard?"; `sim/breaking.ts`). Opus marks what breaks with `a.breakable(build, { strength })`, so the designer, who knows what holds the place up, decides; everything built inside breaks together, so what sits on a shelf goes with it. Breaking everything by its material was turned down: it would leave decorations floating, take floors from under spawn points and bring down structure the designer never meant to lose.
  - A blow counts its energy in joules: a body, a loose thing or a doctor knocked flat striking the piece brings ½mv² (each striker counts once in 0.3 s, so a ragdoll's parts count once); a blast brings ½ · 80 · (its speed there)², as if it threw a doctor, reaching the piece's nearest point unless the arena shelters it. Blows under a twentieth of its strength leave no mark, the rest add up (a mark is chips and a crunch). Doctors walking into a piece never break it: standing doctors touch nothing as bodies do.
  - Strength by default from the biggest solid's material and the piece's volume to the 2/3 (glass 150 J for a cubic metre up to metal 8000): a pane of glass about 50, a crate 800, a bookcase 3200, a stone wall 7000. A thrown thing strikes with about 250, a doctor flung into a wall 2000 to 10000.
  - Broken, its colliders go and it falls apart into rubble, at most 24 lumps a piece and 120 in the arena: bodies cut unevenly from each solid (up to four along a side, at least two along the longest), each 0.88 of its cell so lumps start apart and nothing jams against what the piece stood flush with (a bookcase against a wall, under a balcony, first stayed standing as rubble), thrown out from the piece's middle and on along the blow, in its surface on the page, tumbling over the arena and through doctors, gone after 6 to 8 s; its decorations fly off whole (unless over 3 m). A breakable piece standing on its top falls whole (one lump a solid, no crash of its own). The page folds the piece out of the merged meshes by moving its corners to one point, so nothing is merged again; the arena view carries the broken pieces for a page that comes in late, and the sound test for walls leaves breakable pieces out.
  - The arena check refuses a spawn point on a breakable piece (a doctor falling out would come back over a hole); lamps, portraits and movers are built outside a piece, and no piece inside a mover. The hall's bookcases, a glass specimen cabinet and a stack of exam-paper crates break.
- **Every thing is tried before it goes into a crate:** used, held, let go, used the other way, walked with, thrown, in the game's own hall with two other doctors; any error sends it back to the designer once.
- **The break:** the next arena starts building when a round starts. When everyone is ready (bots always are), the arena appears with a 5 s countdown (2026-11-09: "when everyone is ready, then the game should start in 5 sec"); 60 s after the first is ready it starts anyway. The arena never holds the start up: when Opus's new one is not done, a kept one is played (the game's hall when none is kept) and the new one comes next round. A player must give a name; an empty thesis title is written by Haiku.
  - While it builds, the dressing room shows a bar against the time a build nearly always takes (2026-11-10: "add some visual marker, maybe a progress bar for building an arena. there's likely a 95% build time quantile we could use?"): the 95th percentile of the last 20 kept arenas' build times, retries and all (180 s then, with the median at 84 s, so most builds end near the bar's middle), 180 s while fewer than 5 are kept. Past it the bar breathes and says longer than usual.
  - Every timer in the lobby (the countdown, the round's and the match's end, the break's wait) is sent again whenever its number changes. Before 2026-11-10 only the dressing room's was sent each second, so the countdown stayed at 5 ("countdown at start doesn't work").
- **Dropping out is forgiven:** a player whose page reloads or whose network blinks in a round keeps their doctor, standing still, for 20 s and plays on when back; the round does not end while they may come back. Before, the doctor went at once and they came back to watch someone else for the rest of the round ("I'm now playing as the bot and my character is immobile").
- **Looks are only looks** (one body to hit for everyone), the wardrobe is shared, profiles live in the player's own browser.
- **The committee's patience** (invented, after bot rounds stalled): past 5 minutes of a round, everyone still in loses 2 a second. It was 150 s until 2026-11-10: "patience needs to be like 5mins at least. opus design takes so much time" (a thing asked for must have time to arrive and be used).
- **The chair** (invented): the first player in the room sets bots and arenas and can end a round.
- **Dev server only**: there is no build; the game plays where the dev server runs.
- **The look is its own:** dark academia (walnut, parchment, brass, serif type) on the page and in the arenas.
- **Detail, not blocks** (2026-11-09: "make the graphics far more elaborate, especially the arena; more details, texture etc."):
  - Every arena solid has a material, painted in the page from tileable noise and layouts into a colour map and a relief (normal) map, tiled in metres by box mapping, so nothing stretches and neighbours meet. Most materials take the solid's colour whole; books, chalkboard and lava keep most of their own.
  - Arenas also have solid pieces of any shape (`a.solid`, model code with a collider made from its triangles), decorations with materials, and up to 12 lamps.
  - The page merges each arena into one mesh per material per mover, so an arena of a thousand pieces is a few dozen draw calls. Boxes are bevelled. A broken piece is folded out of them in place.
  - Lighting: environment reflections (RoomEnvironment), a sun whose shadows are fitted to the arena, and a sky dome (gradient, sun glow, clouds) with fog in the horizon's colour.
  - Finishing passes: ambient occlusion (GTAO, high quality) and bloom, through a multisampled half-float target; with neither, the plain draw. Dust drifts in the light.
  - Settings ("a settings toggle for bloom and fullscreen"), on the pause card and in the dressing room's header, kept in the browser: graphics quality (high, medium, low), bloom on or off, fullscreen, and in a round the mouse speed.
  - Doctors have woven cloth, lapels, pockets with pens, buttons, rounded shoes, ears, eyebrows and a mouth. The dressing room is a robing room of bookshelves on a marble pedestal.
  - The arena prompt asks for a place as rich in detail as a real one, every surface its material, furnished and ornamented, lit; it names the materials and the building blocks and no example objects.
  - Models (things, worn parts, decor) are drawn through the recipe builder (`src/model/mesh.ts`) with a fine grain and glow bright enough to bloom.

- **Weapons work as they should** (2026-11-09: "make sure the weapon physics and function are as expected. A netgun shoots a net that opens and captures for a while, electric baton electrocutes for a second or two anyone hit, with visible animation, cannon shoots a realistic projectile that explodes on impact"). The three were the test cases, nothing in the engine or the prompts names them:
  - Asked for the three twice each, Opus twisted four of six (a human cannon that fires its holder, a conductor's baton throwing lightning): the prompt said to twist clear words "now and then", and a model cannot roll dice. The game now rolls (one in four, `GENIE` in `game.ts`) and tells the designer when to twist.
  - Untwisted, it made historically real solid shot that never exploded. The prompt now says clear words make the thing doing all a player would expect of it from films and games, every movement and effect shown, moving with real weight, momentum and the world's pull; after that every cannon exploded.
  - New building blocks, general: in `w.affect`, speed and jump down to 0, `hands`, `shake`, `glow`, `wear`; `w.held`; for bodies `rot`, `face` (points along its flight), `trail`, and on the body `age()`, `resize(k)` (drawn and as it collides) and `pose`; `hit(target, body)` with the velocity it came in with (the body's own may have bounced already); `w.puff` (clouds; glowing ones are fire and light what is near), `w.shake`, crackling beams (`jag`), glowing bursts; the sounds boom and crackle. A model body's collider now sits round the model, wherever its origin is.
  - Drawn: doctors tremble, at full strength convulse with the arms flung out, and glow (flickering while they tremble); what an effect puts on them is drawn round them (the player's own too, seen from inside), their view shakes and takes the glow's colour at its edges, and the crosshair says Held. Clouds and trails are points with a shader (one draw each); glowing clouds borrow one of two lamps kept in the scene, since a lamp added or taken away makes three.js rebuild every shader.
  - Fixed on the way: what a hit takes floated at the attacker's feet instead of over the victim; doctors held their thing up in the left arm while it was drawn at the right hand.

- **Doctors' bodies** (2026-11-09, after comparing with A Gentlemen's Dispute: "how difficult would it be to create more joints and movement for the doctors?", then "go"):
  - Joints: hips, knees, ankles, a waist, a neck, shoulders, elbows, wrists (`scene/doctor.ts`). Every movement is made in the page from what the snapshots already say. The speed is measured from the drawn positions, and a jump to a new place counts as none.
  - Gait: a stride cycle with bending knees and level feet, faster and lower when running, the hips turned toward where the doctor goes (backwards: legs ahead, walking back) while the chest keeps to the aim.
  - The rest of the body: a tuck in the air and a squash on landing; flinching, struggling when held, trembling; looking about when idle; riding and writing poses.
  - Hands: two-bone IK puts the right hand exactly where the game puts the held thing; a thing over 0.45 m long gets the left hand on it too.
  - Gestures play over it all: the sim sends `act` events (use, alt, throw, shove, pick) and the page plays a recoil, a flick, a throw, a two-handed shove, a stoop; a taunt raises a fist with the other hand on the hip.
  - Bigger (2026-11-10: "all movements, all actions should be larger"): strides, knees, arm swings, bob, leans, the tuck, the squash, flinches, recoils, the throw's swing and the taunt are all about half as big again as first made, and gestures last a little longer.
  - Ragdolls in the game (2026-11-10: "then add the real physics, step 3"): `sim/ragdoll.ts` builds nine Rapier bodies (a rigid trunk of hips, chest and head; upper and lower arms; thighs; shins with feet) joined by ball joints at the shoulders and hinges at the hips, knees and elbows, limited to the ways they bend (Rapier takes a hinge's limits only on the joint once made, not from its description). Their colliders are the doctor's own, so rays, bodies and thrown things find the doctor.
  - When: a knock of 11.5 m/s or more (`FLOP`; a shove is 11, so it only pushes), and every dismissal (away from whoever did it). What is held drops. A knock while down throws the body again. A doctor still in gets up on the floor under the trunk once it has lain still 0.35 s (after at least 0.8 s), or after 3.5 s, and is not knocked flat again for 1.5 s. A dismissed body lies 7 s, then stops touching anything, drops through the floor and is gone at 8.5 s.
  - The snapshot carries the pelvis and each part's turn (each part has the drawn joint's own frame), and the page turns them straight into the joints, blending back into the doctor's own pose over 0.45 s when they get up. While down, the player's own camera pulls back behind their body. The page-made ragdoll and the page's tumble that came before are gone.

- **What things cause, big** (2026-11-10: "the important part of the weapons is that they should have different effects and physical impacts ... what they cause is more important. and the cause should be visually bigger and more clear. i wonder, could we somehow get opus to design more elaborate visuals for the weapon effects? if we don't restrict objects that much?", then "go"). Opus could only combine stock sparks, clouds, beams and words, and could cause little but harm, a push, a slow-down or a hold, so most designs came out as "hit for 4 to 10 and push a bit". General building blocks, nothing named:
  - Sights (`w.show`): any model the thing made, only seen, never touched, not counted among bodies (60 a thing, 400 in all). It swells or shrinks from one size to another (a size may stretch it along an axis), drifts, spins, fades over the last part of its life and lights what is near; it can point its +z along a direction (so a model one metre long, stretched, reaches between two points) and follow a doctor, a body or the thing in hand. Code may steer it each step (`set`, `pose`). The page animates it from one event on its own clock; what code changes goes out once a step, and sights wait until the drawing reaches their snapshot, so one steered alongside a body stays on it. Glowing shapes in a sight add light (additive), and two lamps kept in the scene go to the lit sights nearest the eye.
  - Force (`sim/forces.ts`), acting alike on a doctor standing (their knock), lying (every ragdoll part), a body and a loose thing, by mass: `w.blast` throws everything within a radius outward, weaker toward the edge, lighter things faster, the arena sheltering what is behind it, and returns the doctors it reached and how hard (it takes nothing by itself); `w.field` keeps moving whatever is inside (pull, swirl, lift, wind, drag, in m/s²), following the holder, a doctor, a body, the thing or a mover; `w.tie` is a rope (never longer than its length; reeled in with `setLength`) or a spring (stiffness 0 to 1, from a bungee to a snap) between doctors, bodies, the thing in hand (pulling its holder) and fixed points, drawn sagging as slack as it is, or coiled; `w.topple` knocks a doctor flat whatever the speed; `body.stick(target)` fixes a body to the doctor or body it hit (a doctor's pelvis frame, the trunk while lying; it passes through things meanwhile) or where it is; and every body that hits a standing doctor knocks them by its momentum (its mass and speed toward them against the doctor's 80 kg), so light things barely move anyone and a heavy fast one knocks them flat.
  - Holds widened to match: blinded 0.7 or more, pulled by a rope, or in a field stronger than walking (30 m/s²) counts as held; at 5 s they shake free, and ropes and fields leave them alone for 2 s.
  - Smaller pieces: `w.affect` `blind` and `tint` (their own view covered, the HUD's veil); bursts with `speed`, `secs` and a direction with `spread` (sprays and jets), up to 150 bits; `spawn({ hidden })` (a body seen only through its sights); 24 models a thing; a red flash at the view's edges for whoever is hit; what one step may send of sights, bursts, clouds, beams, words, shakes and sounds is capped at 600.
  - The thing prompt says what a thing causes matters most, more than its name or its look, with a character no other thing has, big and plain to everyone across the arena and to the one it happens to, its sights built from its own models, and its forces as strong as the thing really is. It no longer asks for short code.
  - Fixed on the way: a knock stopped dead whenever Rapier's character controller came up short against the floor for a step (the hall's carpet, 4 cm on the boards), taken for a wall; a knock now stops only at a surface steeper than about 45 degrees.

- **One painted look** (2026-11-10: "is there some ideas we could borrow from the gentleman's dispute in terms of visuals? and aim to make it more coherent", then "do 1-5"). A Gentlemen's Dispute's lead environment artist describes its pipeline: brush strokes painted into the normal map, a light/mid/dark split of the diffuse light, colour grading in the engine, and the same painterly pipeline on nearly every model. Opus is our artist and varies, so the page imposes the look and Opus spends no time on it:
  - `scene/paint.ts`: one stroke texture painted in the page at the first draw (1500 flat-brush dabs in a slowly turning flow, ridged with bristle grooves, each with a tone); `paint(material)` lays it over any standard material from three sides in the mesh's own space (strokes stay on a moving part), shifts each stroke's tone, and splits the sun's and lamps' light into three soft bands that keep its average brightness (the first bands brightened the mid-tones by half and the bloom washed the hall out). Models, arena surfaces, doctors, crates, plain bodies and sights all use it; one program serves them all. `harmonize` pulls colours into an oil painting's range in OKLab (lightness 0.17 to 0.9, chroma at most about 0.12, glow 0.2): every model's colours once when built, solid colours, the doctor's look, effects.
  - The finishing grade (`post.ts`) after tone mapping, on every quality: a little less colour, shadows toward a cool umber-blue and highlights toward cream (the shadow side's hue, as a painter shifts it), a soft curve, darker corners, a canvas weave. The finishing passes are always on now (multisampled above low quality).
  - Effects: clouds (smoke, dust, fire, trails) are flat-brush dabs turned their own way, with bristles, ragged ends and three tones, crisp-edged for smoke and soft for fire; bits are short strokes along their flight on the screen, longer the faster.
  - Trails are smoke, only behind what burns as it flies (2026-11-10: "smoke after projectiles is too thick. and only burning ones like rockets and bombs should have a smoke, not chairs"): the prompt says so, and the library's chairs, ducks, pies and the rest lost theirs, keeping the missiles', the flamethrower's and the exploding cannonballs'. A trail starts at half the body's size (8 to 35 cm across, where it was the whole body, 22 cm to 1.5 m), swells to 2.5 times (was 3.5), is 60% opaque (was 80%), and fades within 2.5 m of the eye, so a throw no longer fills the thrower's view.
  - Portraits (`scene/portrait.ts`): each doctor's look rendered once, three-quarter view lit from high on one side against an umber ground, then smeared along the stroke texture and put through a Kuwahara filter (each pixel takes the calmest of the four patches round it); hung in a gilt frame with knobs and a brass name plaque. `a.portrait({ at, rot, size, rank })` in the arena builder and Opus's reference (rank 0 the match's leader); the hall's placeholder founders became seven real portraits.
- **Two hands** (2026-11-10: "i think we need two weapons at the same time. one for left, one for right mouse button ... suspending, slowing an enemy needs some action to utilize that situation, and another weapon would be it"). Each doctor has a left and a right hand: the left button uses the left hand's thing and the right button the right's (an empty hand shoves); Shift with a click is that thing's other use (what right-click was); E picks up into the left hand when it is empty, else the right, and with both full the left hand's drops; Q throws the left hand's thing and F the right's (R was taken). Bots use whichever hand may still take from their foe and look for a second thing near them. Old designs' lines that say right-click are shown as shift-click; the prompt says each thing is used with the button on its side and may be used together with another. Choices made without asking, open to change: which button is which hand, which hand fills first, Shift for the other use, Q and F.

- **An options screen** (2026-11-10: "can you make an options screen, accessible any time, where a person could change like mouse invert, mouse sensitivity, fullscreen/windowed, resolution, graphics level, shadows, bloom, dlss, etc?", then "skip dlss" and "fxaa etc settings? the usual ones. antialiasing, that is"). - **Lighting to tune, and a dusty book** (2026-11-10: "give me some options to control the lighting. ambient, object, etc. i want to be able to find a good general lighting pattern", and "the draw patterns in the objects is a good direction, but can we make everything look more like a dusty book than sockpuppets?"):

- **Doctors who move like old academics** (2026-11-10: "can you improve the movement on the docs a lot more? clothes, joints, etc. and perhaps doctors aren't so fast, have limps, hunchbacks etc. ragdolling should be far more expressive", then "the weapons should sway as well" and "when the doctors have less credibility, they should hunch more, being overburdened by stress and doubt"):

  - Stairs (the same day: "now the doc can't always walk up the stairs without jumping"): Rapier steps a character up only when it meets a step squarely, so met at a slant it slid along the riser, and the slower walk made it stall longer at each one (eight 0.44 m steps took 3.25 s straight on). Rapier's own step settings only made it worse. Now a walking doctor who is blocked looks just ahead the way they walk, and where there is a riser, room above it and a flat top no higher than 0.5 m, hops up onto it: every test staircase is climbed straight on or at a slant in 1.3 to 1.8 s. The arena reference says 0.5 m.
  - A gait in the look (`Look.gait`: chance, steady, limp, hunched, shuffle, waddle, pompous, doddery; chance picks by the player's id, the same on every screen): steps a second and their length, knee lift and a standing bend, a hunch (or the chest thrown out) with the head held up against it, a hip roll, legs set wide, a lame right leg (a short stiff step, the body dropping onto the good side), a tremble, the arms' swing and how they hang. The head lags the body on springs; the free arms swing with follow-through.
  - Things in hand sway (the stride's bob, a turn's lag, a start's lag, a recoil's kick back and up, a tremble): the doctor's hands reach where it has swayed to, the world draws the thing there, and one's own things before the eyes sway the same way.
  - After a fall: dazed for about two seconds, swaying, the head circling, stars round it.
  - The ragdoll has eleven parts (pelvis, chest, head, arms, legs; the snapshot sends eleven turns): soft springs at the waist and the neck, stiffer the further they bend; limbs looser; for its first moments, and while it moves fast, the arms windmill and the legs pedal (fading with its speed and its time down, shorter for the dismissed), then it goes limp.

- **Sound** (2026-11-10: "could we improve the sound design? make the sounds much much more realistic?", then "1-3, please"):
  - The space (`audio.ts`): the listener is the camera; every sound in the world goes through a lowpass for the air (and, when a fixed solid of the arena crosses the line to the ear, a much lower one and less loudness) into an HRTF panner, and a share of it, larger farther off, into a convolver whose impulse response is made for the arena (`scene/hearing.ts`: Sabine's 0.161 V / (S a) from the arena's bounds, a mean absorption weighted by its solids' surfaces, and the share of open sky over it, from nine rays straight up; early reflections at the walls' distances, a noise tail whose highs die faster in a darker room).

  - The game's own sounds: footsteps on each foot put down, by the floor's material under the doctor (the snapshot now says it) and by the gait (a lame foot treads lightly, a pompous one heavily); landings and a rustle of the coat on a jump; a ragdoll heard hitting the ground when its speed drops; a server `impact` event when a body or a loose thing strikes something at 2.5 m/s or more (by the arena's material, a doctor, or another object; its speed and mass), sounded by material, louder and lower the harder and heavier; a cry in the doctor's own pitch when hurt (not every blow of a flurry) and a yell when dismissed; explosions, cannons and thunder with a drawn rumble under them, and far off the muffled recording.
  - Made things: `w.sound` takes a volume and chooses from 60 names (the old twelve still mean what they meant); the reference says the game already sounds footsteps, landings and impacts.
  - Checked headless: every recording decodes in Chromium (lengths and peaks as expected), and every sound plays through the whole chain without an error. Not yet heard: the levels between sounds want a listen in play.
  - First play ("sound isn't working", "very metallic sounds, the old ones"): no recording loaded. Vite rewrites `new URL('../sounds/', import.meta.url)` without its last slash, so the page asked for `soundslibrary.json` and fell back to the drawn sounds everywhere; the headless check had loaded the library another way. - **Feet** (2026-11-10: "bunny slippers aren't moving with the feet"): there was no slot for the feet, so Opus put slippers in the body slot, which goes with the trunk. - **The page set like the book** (2026-11-10: "let's work on the dressing room screen and make it coherent and consitent. also, make the game popups similar. increase font size and button sizes, and improve the overall visual appear of the non-3d elements", then "the doc in dressing room should have far more realestate"):

- **Clothes that move** (2026-11-10: "can the clothes be made to move?", then "i mean, bend, sway, swing etc."). Only the coat's four panels swung, as stiff plates hinged at the waist; whatever was worn was fixed to its slot.
  - What is worn swings (`scene/swing.ts`): every named part of a worn model hangs from its pivot as a pendulum, its weight at the middle of its shapes and those of the parts inside it (`hangOf` in the kit), thrown by how that weight moves in the world (starts, stops, turns, the step's bob, landings, the ragdoll's flight) and pulled by the world as the doctor bends or lies. At rest a part is as made on a doctor standing upright. A part made hanging straight down is limp (a quarter of the pull's stiffness), so a cape falls toward the ground with a doctor lying down; one made sticking out or up is springy (1.6 times), so it stays out, wobbling.   - The coat's panels bend below the pockets: each hem trails its panel on a softer spring, flaring further with the air, and the panel curves to it, keeping its length.
  - Every copy of a part made under one name is posed now; before, only the last copy of each name moved (of the baubles twinkling as 'light0', one did).

## How it is built

- `protocol.ts`: the messages and the shared numbers. `doctor.ts`: the body plan, the slot frames, the starter wardrobe.
- `sim/`: `sim.ts` (Rapier world, doctors on Rapier's character controller, things, bodies, sights, zones, crates, harm, snapshots), `forces.ts` (blasts, fields, ties, sticking, momentum), `breaking.ts` (breakable pieces and their rubble), `api.ts` (the `me` and `w` made code gets), `arena.ts` (the builder `a` and the checks), `hall.ts` (the game's own hall, the fullest use of the builder), `shapes.ts` (a model's triangles for a solid piece's collider), `things.ts` (designs, compiling, the trial, the four starter things), `bots.ts`, `sandbox.ts`.
- `server/`: `plugin.ts` (the Vite plugin: the WebSocket, the local-address check, reloading the game when its code changes), `main.ts`, `game.ts` (players, phases, crates and designs, dressing, lines, arenas, bots), `opus.ts` (the CLI and the model picker), `prompts.ts` (prompts, API references, schemas), `voice.ts` (each doctor's voice, the tones, the worker) and `voice-worker.ts` (Kokoro speaking, levelling, MP3), `store.ts` (the library).
- The page: `net.ts`, `controls.ts`, `sounds.ts` (synth sounds and the taunts as said), `fonts.ts` (the label face, and waiting for the fonts), `options.ts` (the player's options, kept in the browser), `lighting.ts` (the lighting and the look, to tune), `OptionsScreen.tsx` (the options screen and its lighting tab), `scene/` (`world.ts` keeps the round in three.js from interpolated snapshots, `arena.ts` draws an arena merged by material with its sky, lamps and dust, `materials.ts` paints the surfaces and box-maps geometry, `effects.ts` bursts, beams, clouds, trails and floating words, `sights.ts` sights and ties, `paint.ts` the painted look and colour harmony, `portrait.ts` the doctors' portraits and their frames, `post.ts` the finishing passes, `doctor.ts` the doctor's joints and movement and the ragdoll as drawn, `swing.ts` what is worn swinging, `models.ts`), `Dressing.tsx`, `Round.tsx`, `App.tsx`.
- Tests (`npm test`): the sim's rules, the starter things through the trial, holds and how effects show, bodies that grow, point along their flight and report their hits, crates, disguises and riding, the arena checks, the sandbox;
